# Solstice places dataset

Generated data for [Solstice](https://solstice.bar) — every named café, restaurant and bar from
OpenStreetMap, with whether it has a terrace and the skyline around it, cut into map tiles the app
downloads for the area on screen; and, for Paris's terrace register, the skylines, categories and
names the app reads. Nothing here is edited by hand.

## Two channels

- **`solstice-places-beta`** — built from France's OpenStreetMap extract each month and whenever
  the app's `beta-labo` branch changes, by the app repository's `publish-places.yml`. Read by the
  app's test builds (development, beta, e2e).
- **`solstice-places`** — the App Store app's. Never built: it receives, byte for byte, what the
  beta channel published once testers have validated it (`promote-places.yml`, on the app's `main`),
  and `manifest.json` proves it — both channels serve the same fingerprint.

The processes behind them are described in the app repository's `docs/data-pipeline.md`.

## Layout

```
manifest.json                 every file's SHA-256, one fingerprint over them all, the commit that built them
v1/index.json                 tiles present, their counts, the source extracts and their dates
v1/10/{x}/{y}.json            the places in one zoom-10 slippy-map tile
v1/paris-names.json           names on the sign for Paris's terrace-register places
v1/paris-questions.json       the questions the app asks readers where a name stays in doubt
v1/register-horizons.json     the register permits' skylines, and the points measured outside a building
v1/address-points.json        where each Paris establishment's door stands (BAN): the side a skyline steps out on
v1/register-categories.json   the register permits' categories (café, bar, restaurant…)
v1/lab/                       the Labo's terrace footprints, zoom-15 tiles (test builds only)
v1/ground-truth-check.json    this build against what the team verified: pass or fail, and what to verify again
v1/quarantine.json            OSM's changes against production waiting for a second extract
compare/                      beta channel only: where the sun changes against production, and
                              release.json: what the release changes, the draw the team must verify
```

Each place is a positional array
`[id, name, lng, lat, amenity, terrace, cuisine, opening_hours, street, postcode, city, horizon?, frontage?]`,
described in `v1/index.json`. `id` is the OSM object (`n123`, `w456`, `r789`); `terrace` is
`0` none, `1` confirmed, `2` unknown.

`horizon`, when present, is the skyline around the place: for each of 72 bearings (every 5°,
clockwise from north), the altitude in whole degrees above which the sky is clear within 500 m,
as two hex digits. Computed from OpenStreetMap buildings, simple outlines and courtyard blocks
alike — `height`, else `building:levels` × 3.1 m, else the median of tagged neighbours — from
where someone would sit: the place's point, or 2 m outside the nearest wall when the point is
inside a building. `register-horizons.json` holds the same for the register's permits, computed in
the same pass.

`frontage`, on Paris's places whose OSM tags give no house number, is the Base Adresse Nationale's:
the numbers of the address points within 3 m of the nearest one, nearest first and separated by
spaces — a café on a corner has one on each street (« 88 18 »). It tells a café from its neighbour
when the city register is matched against OSM.

`paris-names.json` maps a Paris terrace-register entry (the app's id for it) to the name on its
sign. The register records what the permit holder filed — often a company's name — so where a
permit matches an OSM place by name, the OSM name is given; corrections made by hand from users'
reports override both, and the team's verified corrections (`team-corrections.json`, from the
verification desk through solstice-ground-truth) override everything. `names` holds the map, `how`
where each came from (`team` for the team's), `addresses` the team's verified addresses (the street
line), `enclosed` the terraces the team saw enclosed (the place stays, as a restaurant without a
terrace), `removed` the places it found closed or gone (off the map), `metadata` the counts and
the dataset it came with. `team-corrections.json` is regenerated by solstice-ground-truth's
`tools/export_corrections.py`, never edited: it also keeps every observation behind each
correction (`history`).

## The ground-truth check

Every build is checked against what the team verified on the verification desk
(`scripts/check-ground-truth.ts`, over `ground-truth-checks.json`, exported by
solstice-ground-truth's `tools/export_checks.py`): the team's names and addresses published and
its removed places removed; a register footprint judged right not moved (overlap at least 0.6); a
season where the team found the beta right within a tenth of the beta judged (at least 0.2 h). The report is
`v1/ground-truth-check.json`. A beta that fails it is still published, for testers, but
`promote-places.yml` refuses to send it to production: no correction lost, and at most a tenth of
what was judged right changed. What moved is listed under `reverify`, for the next campaign.

## History: every dataset, every change

Neither channel is force-pushed: each publication is a commit on top of the last, tagged
(`beta-<UTC time>-<commit>`, `prod-<UTC time>-<fingerprint>`), so every dataset served stays
retrievable with `git checkout <tag>`; git keeps unchanged files once. Each build logs what it
changes against the beta it replaces, value by value (`scripts/build-changelog.ts`,
`scripts/lib/dataset-diff.ts`): `history/<build>.jsonl.gz`, one change a line
(`{"kind","id","field","op","from","to"}`), and its line in `history/index.json` — inputs (code
commit, OSM extract, the register's and the team corrections' SHA-256), the build it follows, the
counts, the tag. Beside it, `history/<build>.edits.json.gz` (`scripts/osm-edits.ts`, `edits` in the
build's line): the OSM edits of the weeks before its extract, from Geofabrik's daily diffs — every
version of the elements that are or were places, the buildings edited near them, located — so
which edit made a change stays known after Geofabrik deletes its diffs (100 days). `history/` sits
beside `v1/`, outside the manifest. Production also keeps `releases/`: each promotion's
`compare/release.json`, as `<tag>.json` and `latest.json`, from which the next release's draw
starts. A value's history is the
lines naming its id across builds:

    zcat history/*.jsonl.gz | grep '"id":"t1abc"'

Each published register name has its lineage in `v1/lineage/names.json` (not downloaded by the
app): the claim shown and those it replaced — the register's, OSM's by name, a SIRENE rule's,
the scored matcher's, a hand override, the team's — each with its source (the OSM place's id…),
its score and what the rule saw (distance, name similarity, the candidates weighed).

The other values have theirs in `v1/lineage/` too:

| File | Each record says |
|---|---|
| `categories.json` | the OSM POI that lent the category, by which tier (name, address, proximity), its distance and name similarity |
| `skylines.json` | the cell of buildings it was measured against, and from where (the permit's point, or the pavement and how far) |
| `footprints.json` | how the footprint was drawn (along the façade, or the register's rectangle away from it; the register's dimensions; the wall's distance), or why there is none (display, enclosed, no dimensions, no façade) |
| `frontage.json` | the BAN address points behind an OSM place's house numbers, nearest first (a corner has one per street): label, BAN id, distance |
| `corrections.json` | the observations behind each of the team's addresses, enclosed or opened terraces and removed places |

Ids that last: a permit renamed at the register gets a new id (its id hashes its point and name),
an OSM café redrawn as a building goes from a node to a way. Each build maps the ids that disappear
to those that appear (the same point, the same SIRET a few metres off, the same OSM name close by;
`scripts/lib/id-map.ts`, against the previous build's `v1/lineage/permits.json`), writes the map
to `history/<build>.ids.json`, and logs one change of id instead of a removal and an addition; the
ground truth's exports follow it. Each changelog line for a name, a category, a footprint or a
skyline carries `why`: the new value's lineage (rule, source, score, what the rule saw), so a
change and its evidence are read together — what solstice-ground-truth's `tools/evaluate.py`
scores against the team's verdicts.

## The numbers against production

Before a beta is published, `scripts/check-build-metrics.ts` compares its headline numbers with
production's — OSM places, those with a terrace, a skyline, a street number; register names in
all and by source (OSM, SIRENE rules A and C); register skylines and categories; footprints and
places measured on the pavement — and writes `v1/build-metrics.json`. A number that falls by more
than 2 % (of a base of at least 100) stops the build, as does one the beta no longer has, unless
`expected-drops.json` declares it: the key, why, and until when. Promotion refuses a beta without
a passing report. On 2026-10-21 a loader change lost 1,003 register names (from OSM 10,579 →
9,556) and every check passed; this one fails on it.

The workflow rebuilds on any change to what the build's scripts (`scripts/places-build.json`)
import — `src/lib/**`, `src/data/terrasses/**`, `scripts/lib/**` —, and promotion compares every
one of those files (`scripts/stage-key.ts --list`) between the beta's commit and main's;
`scripts/lib/workflow-paths.test.ts` fails when a script imports a file the triggers miss. The
matching itself is replayed on a recorded sample (`scripts/lib/matching-golden.test.ts`).

## What a release must have verified

`compare/release.json` (`scripts/release-changes.ts`, run by compare-sun.yml) lists what the beta
changes against production — sun beyond 10 %, footprints new or redrawn, places new or gone,
names, addresses —, by cause (the mechanism that made the change), and a stable weighted random
draw of each: enough cases for the share of a cause's changes that are wrong to be bounded under
10 % at 95 % (`release-policy.json`). The verification desk asks the draw (« Avant la
production »); `promote-places.yml` refuses the beta until every case drawn is verified within the
rule. A wrong change the team corrected (name, address, terrace, status) does not count against
it. Since version 3 (2026-11-06, docs/plan-release-validation.md):

- the bound is the finite population's: a cause of 46 changes needs 20 cases with no error, one of
  665 needs 28;
- a cause whose code is production's (a monthly refresh of OSM only) weighs its visible changes
  by where they are read (Paris twice) and how risky their edit is, counts a changeset behind ten
  changes or more as one unit (three of its changes verified), and starts with half of what
  production's last release verified for it (`releases/latest.json`);
- automatic checks — a name changed only in case or punctuation, an address the BAN puts at the
  place, a closure its mapper declared, an active establishment of that name in SIRENE, buildings
  at BD TOPO's height — take a change out of the draw once the team has verified enough of their
  passes, but for one in ten (`checks` in the report: each check's ledger);
- `upcoming` draws what the quarantine holds as the next release will, for the team to verify
  while it waits.

## OSM's changes wait for a second extract

A place OSM added, removed or renamed since production reaches the tiles only when an extract at
least a week later still has the change (`scripts/quarantine-osm.ts`, after the skylines). On the
first release checked, one change in seven was OSM's own error — a café deleted though open, one
added though closed, wrong renames — and such edits are often reverted within weeks. Until then
the tiles keep production's place, and `v1/quarantine.json` lists each change waiting (`pending`:
id → `kind` new, gone or name, the extract it was first seen in, `from`, `to`, and where the place
stands: `at`, its street, postcode, city and kind), which the next build reads back from the beta
channel, and the release's draw shows the team ahead (`upcoming`). A place that only changed id (redrawn from a node to a
way) passes at once (`redrawn`: old id → new). A change confirmed stays listed, with `confirmed`
(the extract that confirmed it), until production has it: every build compares with production
again, and would otherwise hold it once more. Should a build lose that record, a dispatch with
`quarantine_from` (an older gh-pages commit of the beta, through raw.githubusercontent.com) gives
back the extract each change was first seen in. In practice, OSM's changes reach production a
month after they appear, with the next monthly extract.
